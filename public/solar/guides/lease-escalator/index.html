Solar Lease and PPA Escalators: What a 2.9% Annual Increase Really Costs

An escalator clause raises your solar lease payment or PPA rate every year. A few percent sounds small. Compounded over 25 years, it isn't.

Updated October 2026 · 5 min read · By Your Solar Advisor

Short answer

An escalator raises your payment every year. At 2.9%, a $150 lease payment becomes about $298 a month by year 25 — nearly double — and you pay about $64,800 in total instead of $45,000 with no escalator. Ask for 0–1.9%, or compare the total cost before you sign.

How escalators compound

Each year's increase is applied to the previous year's payment, not the original one. That's why the payment grows faster as the years go on.

EscalatorYear 1Year 2525-year total
0%$150/mo$150/mo$45,000
1.9%$150/mo$236/mo$56,924
2.9%$150/mo$298/mo$64,772
3.9%$150/mo$376/mo$73,961

A PPA works the same way on its per-kWh rate: 20¢ per kWh rising 2.9% a year becomes about 25.9¢ in year 10 and 39.7¢ in year 25.

“But utility rates go up faster”

That's the usual pitch, and sometimes it's true. Utility rates in some states — California especially — have risen faster than 3% a year recently. In other places they've risen more slowly, and they can stay flat for years.

An escalator is a fixed bet that lasts 20–25 years. If your utility's rates rise more slowly than the escalator, your savings shrink every year — and can turn into a loss in the later years. Meanwhile your panels produce a little less each year (about 0.5%), even as the payment climbs.

A higher flat payment can cost less

Some companies offer a choice: a lower starting payment with an escalator, or a higher payment that never changes. Over 25 years, a flat payment of up to about $216 a month costs less than $150 rising 2.9% a year. A flat $185, for example, totals $55,500 — about $9,300 less.

What to negotiate

  • Ask for a 0% escalator option, even if the starting payment is higher. Then compare the totals.
  • If there must be an escalator, push for 1.9% or less.
  • Check the contract length — 20 and 25 years make a big difference to the total.
  • Get the 25-year total cost in writing, not just the year-1 payment.

Red flags

  • The escalator is in the fine print, not on the first page.
  • Savings projections that assume your utility rates rise 5% or more every year.
  • A comparison that only shows year 1: “you'll pay less than your current bill.”
  • An escalator above 2.9%.
